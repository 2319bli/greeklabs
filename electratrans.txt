Section 1
Paidagogos ​
O child of Agamemnon, who once commanded at Troy, ​
now at last you may look upon what you have always longed to see. ​
For this is that ancient Argos you have desired, ​
the grove of Inachus’ daughter, stung by madness.
This, Orestes, is the marketplace of the wolf-slaying god, Apollo Lykeios; ​
to the left is the glorious temple of Hera. ​
And here, where we have come, they say are the gold-rich Mycenae, ​
and this is the ruinous house of the Pelopids, ​
from where I once took you away after your father’s murder
and carried you to the side of your sister and brother, ​
saving and raising you until you reached this youth, ​
so that you might become your father’s avenger. ​
Section 2

Now then, Orestes, dearest of men, and you, beloved Pylades,
we must quickly decide what is to be done. ​
For already the bright light of the sun is moving, ​
the morning cries of birds are clear, ​
and the dark night has left the sky. ​
Before any of the household come forth,
take counsel together; for now is no time to delay, but the moment for action.​

Orestes 

Dearest of servants, how plainly you show your loyalty to us! ​
For just as a noble horse, though old, ​
does not lose his spirit in danger,
but pricks up his ears, ​
so you urge us on and yourself take the lead. ​
I will now set out my plan; and you, listening keenly, ​
if I miss the mark, correct me. ​

Section 3

When I came to the Pythian oracle,
to learn how I might avenge my father’s murderers, ​
Phoebus gave me this command, as you shall hear: ​
without shield or army, by deceit, ​
I must carry out the just slaughter with my own hand.
Since such was the oracle, ​
you, old man, shall enter the house when the time comes, ​
learn everything that happens, ​
and report it clearly to us.​
Your old age will protect you,
and they will not suspect you, so grey and harmless. ​
Say this: that you are a stranger from Phocis, ​
sent by Phanoteus (their closest ally),
and swear that Orestes is dead, ​
killed in a chariot race at the Pythian games.
Let this story stand firm. ​

Section 4

Meanwhile, we shall crown our father’s tomb,
pour libations and cut locks of hair,
then return again,
carrying a bronze urn,
which you know is hidden in the bushes,
so that we may confirm the tale that my body​
has been consumed by fire and ashes.
Why should I grieve at this, if by a tale of death
I am saved in deed, and win glory?
I think that there is no word that brings advantage is bad.
I have often seen even wise men die in words in vain,
and when they return home, they are honoured the more.
So too I trust that from this rumour
I shall shine upon my enemies like a star.

Section 5
But, O land of my father and local gods,
receive me having good fortune on these paths,
and you, O home of my father; for I have come
to cleanse you with justice rousing by the gods;
and don't send me dishonoured from this land,
but as a master of one's wealth and a restorer of my homes.
Now I have said these things; and already, old man,
see to it that you are going to make sure of your obligation.
We should go; for it is the right time,
the greatest master of any deed for the men.

Electra

Oh wretched me!

Paidagogos

Ah, I think I hear a servant moaning softly within the door, child.

Orestes

Is it the wretched Electra; shall we wait here
and listen to her wailing?

Paidagogus

No; lets not try anything earlier than Loxias' deeds
and start by doing this,
pouring libations for your father; for these things bring
victory to us and power over the doings.

Section 6
Electra

I feel shame, O women, if I seem to you
to be excessively vexed with many laments.
But the force forces me to do this,
forgive me; for how, someone who was a noble woman,
see so much suffering of one's father, would not do these things?
Are there things which I see by day and night always
thriving rather than decaying?
First the things about my mother, who gave birth to me,
turned out the most hostile; then I live together in the house,
with the murderers of my own father,
and I am ruled at the dictates of them and by the dictates of them
I must take similarly what is in want.
Then what sort of days do you think I spend,
when I see Aegisthus sitting on the throne
of my father, and I look at him wearing
an item of clothing the same as him and at the hearth
pouring libations on where he destroyed him,
and I see the final insult of these people,
the perpetrator to us is sleeping with the wretched
mother, if it is necessary to address her as a mother,
in the marriage bed of my father.

Section 7

She is so reckless that she lives with the polluted man,
fearing no avenging Fury;
but, as though laughing at what she is doing,
observing that day, on which once
she murdered my father through trickery,
on this day she sets up dances and sacrifices
to the gods of deliverance in month-long festivals.
And I, seeing (all this), in my misfortune cry along the
rooftops, I waste away, and I lament over my father’s
most wretched banquet, named after him,
myself to myself; for it is not possible to cry
so much that my heart brings me pleasure.
For this so-called noble lady,
as she speaks, throws such wicked words back in my face:
“O godless pollution, have you alone suffered the death
of a father? Is no other mortal in sorrow?
May you perish cruelly, and may the gods below never
release you from these current lamentations.”

Section 8
She insults these things with arrogance; except when she hears
from someone that Orestes has come; and then madly
she shouts, "Are you not the cause of these things for me?
Is this deed not yours, you who having stolen
Orestes from my hands, smuggled him away?
But know, I tell you, that you will pay the deserved penalty."
She barks such things, in company nearby, she urges you on,
her famous bridegroom present with her,
this man is in all respects feeble, the total disaster,
the one who fights battle together with women.
But I always waiting again for Orestes against them
to arrive as someone who puts an end to it.

Section 9

For by always being about to do something,
he has destroyed both the hopes I have and those I did not yet have.
So in such circumstances, friends,
it is possible neither to be self-controlled
nor to be pious; but in troubles
there is great necessity even to practice bad actions.

Chorus

Come tell us; are you saying these things
while Aegisthus is nearby, or after he has left the house?

Electra

Certainly; for I would not think, if he were nearby,
to wander outside the doors; but now he happens to be in the fields.

Chorus

Indeed then I would approach your words
with greater confidence, if matters are really like this.

Electra

Question me now, since he is away; what do you wish?

Chorus

And indeed I ask you, what do you say about your brother-
that he is coming, or delaying? I want to know.

Electra

He says he is coming; but while saying it he does nothing of what he says.

Chorus

For a man engaged in a great task is accustomed to hesitate.

Electra

And yet I saved him without hesitation.

Chorus

Be confident; he is noble by nature, so as to help his friends.

Electra

I trust this – otherwise I would not have lived long.

Chorus

Say nothing further now; for see coming from the house,
your sister of the same father and same birth,
Chrysothemmis, and from the same mother,
carrying funeral offerings in her hands, such as are customary for the dead.

Section 10

Chrysothemis

Why again do you at the exit of this doorway,
having come out cry out this speech, O sister,
and not even over a long time do you wish to be taught
not to indulge emptiness with pointless anger?
And yet I know this much at least about myself,
that I grieve at our present situation; so that, if
I were to gain power, I would make clear to the people what I think.
But now in bad times it seems good to me to sail with a lowered mast,
and not yet to seem to do anything, but not to do harm.
But I want you to also do such things.
And yet what is just is not as I say,
but as you judge. But if I must live free,
everything belonging to those in power must be listened to.

Section 11

Electra

It is terrible at any rate that you being the child the father from of whom you are born,
to have forgotten him, but to care about the woman that bore you.
For all your warnings to me are
taught by her, and you say nothing from yourself.
For choose one of two things, either to think badly,
or, while thinking, not to have memory of dear ones.
For you are one who says just now, that if you
were to gain power, then you would show hatred to them - 
and while exacting vengeance on behalf of my father in every way,
you are not cooperating and deter the one acting.
Does acting like this not involve cowardice in addition to bad things?
For teach me, or learn from me, what advantage
might come to me from stopping these lamentations.
Do I not live? Badly, I know, but well enough for me.

Section 12

I hurt these people, I attach honours
to the dead man, if there is some gratitude there.
And you, who hates us, you hate in word,
in deed you join the murderers of your father.
I would never, not even if someone intends to bring to me
the gifts you get, over which you now revel,
yield to these people; and for you let rich
tables lie and let life flow around you [in luxuries].
For let my only nourishment be not to cause pain;
and I do not desire to obtain your honour,
nor should you, if you were sensible. And now although it is possible
to be called a child of the best father of all, you are called
a child of your mother. For in this way you will appear bad to very many people,
having betrayed your dead father and your friends.

Chorus

Do nothing in anger by the gods; for with these words
there is an advantage for both of you; if you learn
[how] to use her arguments, and she in turn uses yours.

Section 13

Chrysothemis

I, O women, am somehow accustomed to
the words of her; nor would I ever mention them,
if I did not hear that the greatest evil is coming
against her, which will stop her from long laments.

Electra

Come tell the terrible indeed. For if say
something greater to me of this, then I would no longer protest.

Chrysothemis

But I will tell you all that I know.
For they intend to send you from here,
if you don't stop the weeping, where you will never
look at the light of the sun, and living in a 
sealed roof outside of this land you will sing bad things.
Consider these things, and don't ever blame me
later while suffering. For it is now a good time to show sense.

Electra

Have they really planned to do these things to me?


Chrysothemis

Certainly; whenever Aegisthus comes home.

Section 14

Electra

But may he come because of this at speed.

Chrysothemis

Whom did you curse, O wretched woman, with this word?


Electra
I pray that he come, if he intends to do something of these things.

Chrysothemis

In order that you suffer what? Where are you in your mind?

Electra

In order that I escape from you as far as possible.

Chrysothemis

And you don't have any consideration for your present life?

Electra

For my way of life is fine as to admire it.

Chrysothemis

But it would be, if you knew at least to have good sense.

Electra

And don't teach me thoroughly to be wicked to those dear to me.

Chrysothemis

But I don't teach that; but to yield to those in power.

Electra

You flatter these things; you are not choosing my ways.

Chrysothemis

It is fine however to not fall from lack of sense.

Electra

We will fall, if it is necessary, in order to avenge a father.

Chrysothemis

But our father has a pardon of these things, I know.

Section 15

Electra

These things are praised in the nature of evil.

Chrysothemis

But do you not obey and agree with me?

Electra

Certainly not; I would never be so empty-headed.

Chrysothemis

Then I will go to where I am sent on a journey.

Electra

Where are you travelling to? For whom do you bring these burnt sacrifices?

Chrysothemis

Our father sends me to pour libations for our father.

Electra

How did you say this? Really on behalf of those who is the most hostile of the mortals?

Chrysothemis

She herself killed him; for you mean to say these things.

Electra

Having been persuaded by one of the loved ones? Did these things meet his approval?

Chrysothemis

From fear of the night time, in my opinion.

Electra

O ancestral gods, be an ally [for me] now.

Chrysothemis

Do you have some courage about this fright?


Electra

If you were to tell me the dream, I would have spoken then.

Chrysothemis

But I do not know to say except to the extent of already small things.

Electra

But tell me this; often small words already trip up and set upright mortals.
